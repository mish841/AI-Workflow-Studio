Information Security Policy
Version: 2.0
Effective: 12 January 2026
Owner: Chief Information Security Officer
Classification: Internal
Supersedes: Version 1.0 (4 March 2024)

1. Introduction

Northstar Technologies (“Northstar”) builds analytics software for mid-market customers. This Information Security Policy describes mandatory controls for workforce access, authentication, data protection, and incident handling. All employees and contractors who use Northstar systems must follow it. Product-specific customer login experiences are out of scope except where they store or process customer personal data on Northstar infrastructure.

2. Identities and access

Every person who logs in interactively must have a unique user identifier. Shared or generic accounts are prohibited for interactive use. Non-interactive service accounts are permitted if they cannot be used for human login and are recorded in the identity inventory.

Privileged access — including domain administration, cloud tenant administration, production database administration, and security-tool administration — must be requested in writing. The requestor’s manager and a security owner (Information Security or a named application security owner) must both approve the request before the privilege is provisioned. Privileged users must authenticate with multi-factor authentication; password-only privileged login is prohibited.

All remote access to corporate systems, including VPN, zero-trust access brokers, and cloud consoles used from outside a Northstar office, requires multi-factor authentication.

System owners must review user access at least quarterly, including both standard and privileged roles, and remove access that no longer has a business need.

When Human Resources records a termination, or when a contractor engagement ends, IT must revoke directory, VPN, email, source control, cloud console, and production application access within twenty-four (24) hours.

3. Passwords, lockout, and sessions

Passwords must be at least twelve (12) characters. Users should prefer passphrases. Identity and Access Management is encouraged to check new passwords against known-breach datasets when the identity platform makes that check available; the check is recommended, not mandatory, in this version of the policy.

Reuse of any of a user’s last twelve (12) passwords is prohibited, and the identity system must enforce that history check at password change.

After five consecutive failed login attempts, the account must be locked until Identity and Access Management unlocks it or until an automated cool-down of at least fifteen minutes has elapsed.

Privileged sessions must time out after fifteen (15) minutes of inactivity. Standard user sessions must time out after thirty (30) minutes of inactivity. Timeout must require re-authentication; a device screensaver alone is not sufficient for cloud consoles or web applications.

4. Protecting data

Northstar classifies information as Public, Internal, Confidential, or Restricted. Customer personal data is Restricted.

Confidential and Restricted data at rest — including servers, databases, and object storage, not only laptops — must be encrypted using AES-256 or another algorithm approved in writing by Information Security. Encryption keys must not be stored in plaintext beside the data they protect.

Confidential data transmitted over public networks must use TLS 1.2 or higher.

Backups that contain Confidential data must be encrypted and stored offsite or in a geographically separate region from the primary production copy.

5. Detecting and handling incidents

Sev 1 security incidents must be reported to the Security Operations Center within one hour of detection. All other suspected security incidents must be reported by the next business day.

The SOC must assign a documented severity of Sev 1, Sev 2, Sev 3, or Sev 4 within two hours of the report.

If Northstar confirms a confidentiality breach involving a customer’s personal data, that customer must be notified within seventy-two (72) hours of confirmation, unless counsel and the CISO record a legally required delay.

After-action reviews are required for Sev 1 incidents and must be completed within ten (10) business days of closure. After-action reviews for Sev 2 incidents are encouraged. Reviews should capture root cause and follow-up owners when they are performed.

6. Policy administration

This policy is reviewed at least annually and after any Sev 1 incident. Exceptions require time-bounded CISO approval. Questions should be sent to security@northstar.example.
